About

I'm a second-year PhD student at a Dutch university, working at the intersection of health economics, causal identification, and data engineering. My research focuses on the Dutch healthcare system — using administrative microdata, web-scraped registries, and large-scale text data to study how market structure and regulation affect patient outcomes.

Before academia, I developed a taste for building things from scratch: pipelines, databases, scrapers. That habit carries into my research, where the data infrastructure is often as novel as the empirical question.

Research

Commercial Chain Consolidation in General Practice and Patient Outcomes
Joint with supervisors · Netherlands
Studies the effect of private chain takeovers of GP practices on patient outcomes, using staggered difference-in-differences (Callaway–Sant'Anna / Sun–Abraham). The data pipeline — combining Vektis.nl registry data, Orbis commercial ownership records, and Neo4j graph analysis to identify chain takeover events — is independently constructed and methodologically novel.
In progress
Structured Evidence from Dutch Health Litigation: An LLM Pipeline Approach
Solo · Job Market Paper
Builds a large-scale structured dataset from Dutch administrative health litigation records using a web scraping and LLM extraction pipeline. The resulting dataset enables causal analysis of legal and regulatory mechanisms in healthcare — combining econometrics with modern NLP in a domain where structured data has not previously existed.
